Open Safari and look at the tab button. Fifty. Two hundred. At some point the number stops being a number and becomes a "+", which is the iPhone's polite way of saying it has given up counting.

You know what's in there, roughly. An article. A flight you didn't book. Three variations of the same pair of shoes. A recipe. Two pages from a job you finished in June. You keep meaning to sort it out, and instead you open a new tab, because scrolling the grid to find an old one takes longer than searching again.

This guide is about emptying that grid without the small panic that usually stops you — what the built-in options actually do, and a ten-minute triage that ends with an empty Safari and nothing lost.

## Why the tabs are really open

It isn't laziness, and it isn't that you forgot to close them.

A tab stays open because closing it feels like a decision you can't undo. Somewhere underneath is a quieter thought: *if I close this, will I actually find it again?* If the answer is "probably not", the tab is the safest place for it. The tab is visible. Bookmarks are a folder you haven't opened in years.

So the pile isn't a browsing problem. It's a saving problem wearing a browsing costume. Which is good news, because it means the fix isn't discipline — it's having somewhere you trust.

## What's actually in your tabs

Before touching anything, it helps to know that a tab grid is usually four different things at once:

- **Read later.** An article, a thread, a long review. It's a tab because you meant to come back today, and today was a while ago.
- **In progress.** A comparison you're midway through, a booking you haven't finished. These are the only ones that genuinely want to stay open.
- **Reference.** A page you'll need once more, for one specific thing — a tracking number, an address, a spec.
- **Done.** It's finished. You just never closed it.

Most grids are about a tenth "in progress" and nine tenths the other three. That ratio is the whole reason this takes ten minutes instead of an afternoon.

## The built-in options, honestly

Safari does have tools for this. Each is good at one part of the job, and it's worth knowing where each one stops.

**Tab Groups** are the best native answer for *in progress*. In the tab overview, tap the group name at the bottom to make a new group and move tabs into it. It's real organisation — but the tabs are still tabs, still a grid you scroll.

**Bookmarks** are the long-term filing cabinet, and if your Safari toolbar shows a **Bookmarks** icon you can bookmark everything at once — touch and hold it and choose **Add Bookmarks for [number] Tabs**. That's one tap to safety, and also one tap to a folder of two hundred page titles with no idea why any of them mattered.

**Reading List** is built for articles you'll read once. It's the closest fit for the *read later* pile, and it's also where that pile goes quiet — no folders, no notes, no way to surface something from three weeks ago.

**Copy Links** gives you the raw list. Open the tab overview, tap the group name at the bottom to bring up the Tab Groups menu, then touch and hold the **[number] Tabs** row and choose **Copy Links**. Paste it into Notes and you have every URL, as a wall of text.

**Automatic closing** stops the pile coming back. In **Settings** → **Apps** → **Safari** (just **Settings** → **Safari** on older versions), find **Close Tabs** and pick **After One Day**, **After One Week** or **After One Month**.

A note on buttons. Safari's toolbar has moved around in recent versions, and on the **Compact** tab layout most of it is hidden behind one control: on iOS 27 that's the **Page Menu** to the left of the address bar, and on iOS 26 it's the **•••** button. If a step below names a button you can't see, it's in there. You can also switch layouts in **Settings** → **Apps** → **Safari** → **Tabs**.

## A ten-minute tab triage

The trick is to make closing reversible *first*. Once you can't lose anything, going fast is easy.

### 1. Take a safety net (1 minute)

Use **Copy Links** as described above and paste the lot into a single note called *Tabs, [today's date]*. If your Safari doesn't offer it, use **Add Bookmarks for [number] Tabs** into a new folder instead. Either way, don't read it. It exists so that the rest of this is risk-free.

### 2. Go through the grid once (6 minutes)

Open the tab overview and work through it without opening anything you don't have to. For every tab that isn't *done*:

1. Open it and tap **Share**. (No Share button? On the Compact layout it's behind the **Page Menu** on iOS 27, or **•••** on iOS 26.)
2. Tap **Save to Kloom**. (Not there? Swipe to the end of the app row and tap **More** — [how to save links from any app on iPhone](/blog/save-links-from-any-app-on-iphone/) has the full setup.)
3. Pick a **Space** — *Read later*, *Work*, *Ideas*, whatever your three are.
4. Add one line saying why: *"For the kitchen quote."* *"The cheaper one, free returns."* *"Section 3 is the useful bit."*
5. Tap **Save**.

That line is the part Safari never had room for. A bookmark remembers the page; the note remembers you.

Two things make this faster than it sounds. Kloom pulls the title, site and preview image itself, so you're only typing the note. And if you save the same page twice — likely, when four tabs are the same pair of shoes — it asks *"This link is already saved in this space. Save it again anyway?"* instead of quietly duplicating it.

Leave the genuine *in progress* tabs alone. You'll finish those today.

### 3. Close everything else (10 seconds)

Touch and hold the tab button — the overlapping squares, or on iOS 27's Compact layout the button at the bottom right — and choose **Close All [number] Tabs**.

It feels worse than it is. Everything worth keeping is in Kloom with a note, and the whole list is still sitting in that note from step one.

### 4. Turn on automatic closing (30 seconds)

Set **Close Tabs** to **After One Week**. This is what stops the grid rebuilding itself — a week is long enough to finish anything real, and short enough that nothing settles in.

### 5. Delete the safety net (next week)

If you haven't opened that note in a week, you never will. Delete it. That's the point of the exercise: the ten links you actually wanted are somewhere you can find them, and the other hundred and ninety turned out to be nothing.

## Finding them again, which is the point

An empty tab grid is only an improvement if the links are genuinely reachable.

- **Search** looks across every Space at once and matches the title, the site and the URL. You rarely remember an article's headline; you almost always remember it was on YouTube, or Zomato, or someone's newsletter. Type the site name.
- **Pin** the two or three you keep returning to, so they sit at the top of their Space instead of being a permanent tab.
- **Notes** show as a small icon on the card, and tapping a link with a note shows the note before opening the page — so you see *why* before *what*.
- **Spaces** keep the piles apart, and the **All** tab shows everything together when you'd rather have one list.

## Keeping the grid empty

The habit is one rule: **when you catch yourself leaving a tab open "for later", save it and close it.** Later is what the tab promises and never delivers.

It's the same rule that empties the [chat with yourself on WhatsApp](/blog/stop-sending-links-to-yourself-on-whatsapp/). A tab, a self-chat and a screenshot are the same move — somewhere to put a thing down in two seconds. They only fail at the second half, which is picking it back up.

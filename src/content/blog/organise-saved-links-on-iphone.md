Saving a link takes two seconds. Finding it again three weeks later is the hard part.

Most people don't have a saving problem — they have a pile. A few hundred links spread across Safari bookmarks, Notes, a chat with themselves and a read-later app they stopped opening. Everything is technically saved, and nothing is findable.

This guide is about the other half: giving saved links a shape that survives a busy month. The ideas work anywhere, and the steps are written for [Kloom](/), a small iPhone app built for exactly this.

If you're still figuring out the saving part, start with [how to save links from any app on iPhone](/blog/save-links-from-any-app-on-iphone/) and come back.

## Start with three buckets, not twenty

The usual instinct is to build a neat little category for every topic: *Design*, *Typography*, *Portfolio inspiration*, *Client work*, *Contracts*… Two weeks later you're standing in front of nine near-identical buckets, unsure which one a link belongs to, and the whole thing quietly stops.

Broad beats precise. Three or four buckets, each obvious at a glance:

- **Read later** — articles, threads and videos you want to sit with.
- **Work** — anything tied to a project or a client.
- **Ideas** — references, inspiration, things you might make.

The test is speed, not tidiness: if you hesitate for more than a second about where something goes, you have too many buckets. A link you can't file is a link you don't save.

In Kloom these buckets are **Spaces** — the tabs across the top of the home screen. The first tab is **All**, which shows every link from every Space together, so there's always one list that has everything. Space names are kept short (up to 20 characters) because they have to read cleanly as tabs. Kloom is free with three Spaces, which is about where most people land anyway; Kloom Pro removes the cap.

## Making a Space

Three ways in, depending on where you are:

- **From home** — tap **+**, then **New Space**.
- **While saving from another app** — in the Share sheet, tap **Save to Kloom**, then **New Space…** under the Space picker.
- **From a link you've already saved** — long-press its card, then **Move to ▸ → New Space…**

That last one is the useful one. You rarely know your categories up front; you notice them when the third link of the same kind shows up.

## Putting a link back where it belongs

Links land in the wrong Space all the time — usually because you saved in a hurry from another app. Long-press any card and you get:

- **Move to ▸** — send it to another Space.
- **Duplicate to ▸** — keep a copy in two Spaces, when a link genuinely belongs to both.

The Space the link is already in shows a checkmark and can't be picked, so nothing looks missing. Both menus end with **New Space…**, so you can invent the right bucket on the spot instead of settling for a wrong one.

## Pin the few you keep coming back to

Out of a hundred saved links, maybe five are the ones you actually reopen — the brief, the doc, the recipe you're cooking this week. Those shouldn't need finding at all.

- **Swipe a card left to right** and tap **Pin** — or long-press it and choose **Pin**.
- Pinned links move to a **PINNED** section above the rest of the list.
- Three show at a time; tap the chevron to see the rest.
- Pinning is per Space, and on **All** every Space's pinned links come first.

Unpin the same way. Treat pins as a short shelf, not a second archive — the moment everything is pinned, nothing is.

## Write one line about why you saved it

This is the habit that pays off most, and it takes four seconds.

Long-press a card, tap **Edit**, and add a note — up to 280 characters. *"For the kitchen quote."* *"Section 3 is the useful bit."* *"Read before Friday's call."*

Two things then happen. The card shows a small note icon, so you can see at a glance which links carry context. And tapping a link with a note opens its details first instead of jumping straight to the page — so you read your own reminder before you re-read the article trying to remember why it mattered.

Future-you is a stranger. Leave them a sentence.

## Let a big Space grow folders

A Space that works well at fifty links starts to feel long at two hundred. That's the moment for **Folders**, which live inside a single Space.

The point of a folder isn't decoration — it's subtraction. A link you put in a folder **leaves the Space's main list** and lives in the folder instead, so the list you scroll every day gets shorter. *Work* stays a short list of live things, while *Invoices 2026* and *Old briefs* sit tucked away as folders at the top.

- **Create one** — open a Space, tap **+**, then **New Folder**. (Not available on **All** — a folder belongs to a Space.)
- **File a link** — long-press its card, then **Add to Folder ▸**, and pick a folder or **New Folder…**
- **File several at once** — long-press a card, tap **Select**, tap the other cards you want, then **Add to Folder** in the bar at the bottom.
- **Manage one** — long-press a folder for **Rename**, **Add links**, **Pin** and **Delete**.

A link lives in at most one folder, so there's never a question of where it went. And deleting a folder never deletes links — they return to the Space's main list.

Folders are part of Kloom Pro, along with more than three Spaces.

## When you still can't find it

Two tools, and they cover almost everything.

**Sort.** The **LINKS** row above your list opens a sort menu: **Newest**, **Oldest**, **Title (A–Z)** and **Favourites first**. Favourites are the star you tap inside a link's details — a lighter mark than a pin, and worth using for the handful you'd hate to lose.

**Search.** Tap the magnifier at the top right. Search is global: it looks across every Space at once, folders included, so the tab you happen to be on doesn't matter. It matches the title, the site and the URL.

That last bit is the trick most people miss. When you can't remember what a thing was called, you almost always remember where it came from — so type `youtube`, `figma` or `nytimes` and let the site name do the work.

Two smaller things keep the list readable: a **NEW** tag marks links you haven't opened yet (it clears when you open one, or after 48 hours), and **Compact links** in Settings switches between a tighter card and a taller one with more room for detail.

## A two-minute tidy, once a week

Organising isn't a weekend project. It's a couple of minutes on a Sunday:

1. Open **All** and sort by **Newest**.
2. Anything sitting in the wrong Space — **Move to**.
3. Anything you'll genuinely reopen — **Pin**. Anything already dealt with — delete it.
4. Anything worth keeping but not urgent — give it a one-line note and leave it alone.

Four passes down a week's worth of links. That's the whole system.

## Deleting is safer than it looks

The reason most piles grow is that deleting feels risky. It isn't here.

Deleted links go to **Trash** (Settings → Trash), where they wait before being removed for good — 7 days on the free plan, 30 days on Pro. You can recover anything inside that window. And deleting a **Space** doesn't delete its links: they stay under **All**, so restructuring never costs you anything.

## Taking your links with you

A links app shouldn't be a trap. Kloom Pro can export everything — Settings → **Export links**, or select a few links on home and tap **Export**. Two formats: **HTML bookmarks**, which imports into Chrome, Safari and most read-later apps, and **CSV** for Numbers, Excel or Notion.

## Start small

You don't need a system. You need three Spaces, a handful of pins, and the habit of leaving yourself a sentence.

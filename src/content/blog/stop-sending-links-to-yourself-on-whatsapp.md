Scroll up in your WhatsApp chat with yourself. A recipe from March. Three YouTube videos. A flat listing. A screenshot of an address, a voice note, a shopping list, and somewhere in between, the article you've been meaning to read for a month.

Almost everyone has this chat. It's the fastest place on the phone to drop something, so it becomes the place for everything, and eventually a place where nothing can be found.

This guide explains why the habit breaks down for links, what a link actually needs instead, and how to move to [Kloom](/), a small iPhone app for saving links, without losing the speed that made WhatsApp appealing in the first place.

## Why the self-chat works at first

It's worth being fair to it, because the reasons it's popular are real:

- **It's always open.** WhatsApp is already on your screen ten times a day.
- **It's fast.** Paste, send, done. No decisions.
- **It's everywhere you are.** WhatsApp's Message yourself chat shows up on your linked devices too, so a link sent on the phone is there on the laptop.

For a quick note, or moving a photo from your phone to your computer, it's hard to beat.

## Where it breaks down for links

The trouble starts around the fiftieth link.

- **It's one long timeline.** Links sit in the order you sent them, mixed in with photos, voice notes, documents and half-written reminders. The only way to browse is to scroll.
- **Nothing has a place.** A work doc sits next to a recipe next to a meme. There's no way to say "these are for the trip" and keep them together.
- **You lose the why.** You remember saving *something* about the kitchen quote. You don't remember which of the four links it was, or what you meant to do with it.
- **Search needs the exact words.** WhatsApp's search finds text in messages, so it only helps if you remember how the message was written.

None of this is WhatsApp's fault. It's a messaging app, and it's doing messaging. A chat is built for conversations that move forward. A collection of links needs to stay still and stay sorted.

## What a saved link actually needs

A better home for links doesn't have to be complicated. It needs to do four things:

1. **Save from anywhere in about two seconds**, the same way sending to yourself does, or you'll drift back to the old habit.
2. **Give links a place**: a few broad groups like *Read later*, *Work* and *Ideas*, instead of one timeline.
3. **Keep a line of context**: why you saved it, in your own words.
4. **Find it again by what you remember**: usually the site it came from, not the exact title.

## Saving a link to Kloom instead

In Kloom, saving uses the iPhone **Share** button, which almost every app has. Where you'd have tapped Share → WhatsApp → your own chat, you now tap Share → Kloom.

1. In any app, tap **Share** on the thing you want to keep.
2. Tap **Save to Kloom** in the Share sheet. If you don't see it, swipe to the end of the row of apps and tap **More**. [How to save links from any app on iPhone](/blog/save-links-from-any-app-on-iphone/) shows how to pin it to the front, and where Share hides in each app.
3. Choose a **Space**, and add a note if you want: *"For the kitchen quote."* *"Watch before Friday."*
4. Tap **Save**.

That's the same two seconds, and the link lands in the place it belongs, usually with its title, site and a preview image.

## Saving links people send you on WhatsApp

A lot of links arrive *in* WhatsApp: a friend's recommendation, a group's shared doc, a reel your cousin insists you watch. You don't need to forward them to yourself anymore.

- **Open it, then share it.** Tap the link in the chat to open it, then use **Share** on the page that opens and tap **Save to Kloom**.
- **Or copy it.** Touch and hold the message, tap **Copy**, then in Kloom tap **+**, then **Add Link**, and paste the link in. You can add a note there too.

If a link came with context worth keeping (*"this is the place I told you about"*), put it in the note. That context is exactly what gets lost in a chat.

## Moving the links you already have

Don't try to move the whole chat. Most of those links have done their job, and moving them all just recreates the pile somewhere new.

1. Open your chat with yourself and tap your name at the top to open the chat's info.
2. Go to **Media, links and docs** and switch to **Links**. This shows only the links, newest first, without the photos and notes in between. (WhatsApp's labels change now and then; it lives in the chat's info.)
3. Scroll through the last month or two. For each link you'd genuinely open again, open it and save it to Kloom with a one-line note.
4. Ignore the rest. If it mattered, it'll come back.

The same trick works for an overflowing Safari tab bar, which is the other place links go to wait: [how to close your Safari tabs without losing them](/blog/close-safari-tabs-without-losing-them/) walks through it.

If you save something twice by accident, Kloom notices. It asks *"This link is already saved in this space. Save it again anyway?"*, so a migration never leaves you with duplicates.

## Finding it again

This is where the switch pays off.

- **Spaces** keep links apart. The **All** tab shows everything together when you'd rather see one list.
- **Search** looks across every Space at once and matches the title, the site and the URL. Can't remember what a thing was called? Type `youtube`, `instagram` or `zomato` and let the site name do the work.
- **Notes** show as a small icon on the card, and tapping a link with a note shows it before opening the page, so you see *why* before *what*.

For a fuller setup (pins, folders, a weekly tidy), see [how to organise your saved links on iPhone](/blog/organise-saved-links-on-iphone/).

## Keep the self-chat for what it's good at

This isn't about deleting the chat. It's still the best place for a quick reminder, a photo you're sending to your laptop, or a voice note to yourself.

Just let links live somewhere built for them. The self-chat stays short and useful, and the links stay findable.

[Kloom](/) is free to start, with unlimited links, three Spaces and iCloud sync, and it runs on iPhone with iOS 17 or later. The next time your thumb heads for your own WhatsApp chat, tap **Save to Kloom** instead. Future-you will know where to look.
